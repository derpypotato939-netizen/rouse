# Lane A — ten scripts

Faceless, sound-forward. **The hook is the gimmick, not the argument.** Nobody downloads an alarm
because of a citation, so the research never leads — it only ever shows up as a *surprising fact*,
never as a case being made.

**Production:** `node --experimental-strip-types tools/render-sounds.mjs --count 30 --out ./out` for
audio; a screen recording of the Sound Lab is the visual. CapCut. Under 25 seconds unless noted.

**The two things that must stay true:** nothing claims a health effect (see `docs/SCIENCE.md` — it
is not marketing any more, it is the line you don't cross), and rarity is never described as
something you can buy or grind for.

---

## 1. The pull *(the format to repeat forever)*

Silence. Tap. A sound rises. Badge lands: **LEGENDARY**.

> **On screen, after:** "1 in 78."

No voiceover. No explanation. This is the whole product in eight seconds, and it is infinitely
re-makeable — every pull is a new video.

---

## 2. Thirty in thirty seconds

Thirty generated sounds, one second each, cut on the beat. Rarity badge flashing on each.

> **End card:** "None of these existed before I pressed the button. None of them will happen again."

The most re-postable thing on the list.

---

## 3. Legendary hunt

Pull repeatedly on camera until a Legendary lands. Keep the failures in — that's the tension.

> **On screen:** "23 pulls."

Works because the payoff is genuinely uncertain and genuinely audible.

---

## 4. What makes one rare

Show a Legendary's trait tags appearing one at a time: *piercing · breakneck · accelerating ·
cavernous.*

> **VO:** Four unusual things at once. That's what makes it rare — you can hear every one.

**Why it matters:** proves the rarity isn't decoration. This is the video that stops people calling
it fake.

---

## 5. Common vs Legendary, back to back

Play one of each. Say nothing. Let the difference do the work.

If viewers can't hear it, the mechanic is broken and this video will tell you before your users do.

---

## 6. "Someone patented this in 1977"

Show the patent header — US 4,060,973, *Automatic variable-sound alarm clock*. Eight sounds rotating
so you don't get used to one.

> **Close:** "Forty-nine years ago. Nobody built it properly."

A genuinely surprising artifact, delivered as a curiosity — not as evidence.

---

## 7. Your alarm didn't get quieter

> **On screen:** "Your alarm didn't get quieter. You got used to it."

Three seconds of a stock buzzer, then a generated sound.

The single best hook on this list. It names something everyone has experienced and nobody has words
for. **No citation on screen** — it lands harder as an observation than as a finding.

---

## 8. Set it as your actual alarm

Screen recording: generate, download, set as your phone's alarm.

> **Caption:** "You can use these right now, free. They just won't change tomorrow."

Gives something real away, and every download is a Gate 1 metric.

---

## 9. The eight seconds you don't notice

Waveform, ramp highlighted.

> **VO:** It starts almost silent and climbs for eight seconds. Getting blasted awake at full volume
> feels awful and you stay groggy longer.

A decision made against the obvious choice. Reads as care, not as science.

---

## 10. Pull for someone else

Generate a sound, send the link to a friend on camera, they open it and hear the same one.

> **Caption:** "You can send someone a sound. They get the exact one you pulled."

Directly demonstrates the share loop — the mechanism by which any of this actually spreads.

---

## Notes

- **#1, #2 and #3 are the engine.** Make them in volume; they cost minutes each.
- **#7 is the best cold hook.** Lead with it when you want reach rather than depth.
- Sound on is the point — caption anyway, feeds autoplay muted.
- Every video ends on the URL. Nothing else.
- **Never say a sound is rarer because you paid.** It isn't, and the moment that's implied, the
  mechanic becomes a loot box and the App Store becomes a problem.
