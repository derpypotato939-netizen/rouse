# Brief for advocates

*Send this once, in writing, to anyone posting about Rouse. Keep their reply.*

Thanks for helping. This is short because there are only four rules, and all four are non-negotiable.

## What you're showing

**Your morning, with the app in it.** Wake up, the sound plays, you get up. That's the whole brief.
It should look like a normal day-in-the-life, because that's what makes it worth watching.

You do **not** need to explain how it works, and you should not try. That's my job on the main
account — where every claim carries its source on screen. If explanations come from you, the sourcing
falls apart and the claim becomes mine to defend anyway.

## The four rules

**1. Say you're connected — out loud, in the video.**

Something like *"my friend made this and gave me it free"* in the first few seconds. Not the caption,
not a hashtag at the bottom — spoken or on screen where nobody can miss it.

This is a legal requirement, not a preference. The FTC requires disclosure of any material
connection, and free access plus friendship both count. It's on me if it's missing, so please don't
make me chase it.

**2. No health claims.**

Don't say it fixes sleep, cures grogginess, is scientifically proven, or helps with ADHD. Not because
they'd be exaggerations — because saying them turns a wellness app into something regulated, and that
is a genuinely serious problem.

*Fine:* "this thing actually gets me up." *Not fine:* "this fixed my sleep."

**3. No discipline framing.**

Skip "just wake up earlier", "stop being lazy", "no excuses", 5am-grindset energy. A big part of the
audience physically struggles to wake up, and that framing tells them it's a character flaw. It isn't,
and they'll correctly read the brand as not for them.

The framing that works: *this is a tool for a brain that gets used to things fast.*

**4. Don't fake it.**

If it doesn't wake you up, say so — I'd genuinely rather know. A video of it failing is more useful to
me than a video of it working, because I can fix the first one.

## What to send me first

A quick message confirming you've read this. If you're under 18, I need a parent or guardian to
okay it too — that's my rule, not the law's, and I'm not flexible on it.

## Things that make a video better

- Real morning, real alarm time. Staged 6am when you actually woke at 10 reads as fake instantly.
- Leave the sound audible. It's the whole product, and it's different every day — that's the hook.
- The five-digit number on screen is that specific sound's ID. Worth pointing at.
- Under 30 seconds beats over 30 seconds, nearly always.

## Questions

Ask me. If you're unsure whether something crosses a line, it probably does — send it to me first and
I'll tell you in five minutes.
